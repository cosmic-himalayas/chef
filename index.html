CHEF

Cosmic Himalayas Extended Field

Deep multiwavelength imaging around an extreme quasar concentration at z ≈ 2.2

CHEF covers about 1 deg2 around the Cosmic Himalayas, a concentration of 11 quasars at z = 2.16–2.20 and the densest quasar overdensity in the ~10,000 deg2 SDSS footprint. Its master catalog, CHEF2026, combines 24 bands from GALEX far-ultraviolet to JCMT/SCUBA-2 850 µm.

Catalog

CHEF2026

Sources detected independently in the optical, near-infrared and narrowband images are cross-matched into one catalog with uniform photometry and measured completeness. The optical catalog reaches 5σ depths of 25.6–26.3 mag in ugri.

Measurements

  • Photometric redshifts from LePhare and EAZY, with redshift probability distributions
  • Stellar masses and star-formation rates from CIGALE
  • Rest-frame colors
  • Color-selected star-forming and passive galaxies (BX, BM, LBG, gzK, red galaxies)
  • A compilation of 6,237 spectra, with spectroscopic quasars
  • Hα and Lyα emitters and structure-membership classes
  • Submillimeter sources

Explore the field in your browser

Zoom from the whole field to a single galaxy, compare its images from the far-ultraviolet to 850 µm, and click to open its photometry, redshift and stellar mass. The preview covers 452,039 objects and needs no account.

Images

The 21 broad-band images with their usable-area masks, Legacy Surveys, unWISE and DSS2 color views, and up to four linked panels.

02

Objects

A card for every object: photometry, the spectral energy distribution with its best-fit models, redshift probability, stellar mass, star-formation rate and rest-frame colors.

03

Selections

Objects chosen by redshift, magnitude, color, mass or star-formation rate, alone or within a color-selected sample, drawn on the sky.

04

Plots

A scatter plot of any two quantities of the whole catalog, with the current selection highlighted.

In this preview the viewer withholds, for every object, the information that identifies members of the structure at z ≈ 2.18: the narrowband measurements, the emitter samples and the spectroscopic redshifts of galaxies at z = 2.1–2.3.

CHEF is built on observations with Subaru (HSC, MOIRCS), CFHT (MegaCam), the Blanco telescope (NEWFIRM) and JCMT (SCUBA-2), and on public data from the HSC-SSP, GALEX, Spitzer, WISE, Herschel and SDSS. The full acknowledgements are given in CHEF I.
